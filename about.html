Our story

One classroom. One conviction.

Luis Alberto Urbiola was a secondary-school English teacher who believed that language, technology and a degree were the three levers capable of changing a Peruvian family's trajectory in a single generation.

In March 1994 he rented a room on Avenida Larco and taught nineteen students. Ten years later the institute had a computing school. In 2011 it received its first university licence. Today the institution he founded graduates more than three thousand professionals every year — and the original conviction has not changed at all.

He still teaches one C2 conversation group every Thursday evening. He says it keeps him honest.

Milestones

How we grew

  • 1994

    The institute opens

    Nineteen students, one classroom, one teacher on Avenida Larco.

  • 1999

    First Cambridge cohort

    Forty-two candidates sit FCE; thirty-nine pass.

  • 2004

    School of Computing founded

    Two laboratories, thirty-six machines, an Office and networking curriculum.

  • 2009

    Authorised Cambridge Assessment centre

    Centre code PE-084, still active today.

  • 2011

    University licence granted

    First three bachelor degrees: Administration, Accounting and Systems Engineering.

  • 2016

    Virtual campus launched

    Fully online degrees begin; 4,000 students enrol in the first year.

  • 2019

    SUNEDU licensing renewed · SINEACE accreditation

    Resolution 148-2019 confirms institutional quality standards.

  • 2025

    Luis Urbiola Intelligence

    Ten AI assistants deployed campus-wide; 50,000th graduate celebrated.

What we stand for

Four principles, applied literally

Excellence

Every syllabus is externally reviewed annually. Any course rated below 4.5 by students is rebuilt, not defended.

Access

40% of our students hold a scholarship. Nobody has ever been asked to leave for a temporary inability to pay.

Innovation

We adopted the virtual campus in 2016 and AI tutoring in 2025 — both before they were expected of us.

Community

The legal clinic, the free English programme for public-school teachers and the tech bootcamps for women returning to work are all permanent.
